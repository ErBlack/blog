We’ll start drawing the browser with an empty rectangle.

Despite the whims of design and fashion, in 2026 you can still recognize a browser by the address bar and the back/forward buttons. Let’s add them to the rectangle.

These days a site without an `s` after `http` looks suspicious at the very least, and every browser highlights that somehow—usually with a shield or a padlock. This icon next to the address means the site is running over `https`, which means everything you send it and everything it sends back to you is encrypted, and that’s more or less protected. Pay attention to it: an encrypted connection is the key to healthy web surfing.

Next you can add window controls to taste and a scrollbar on the right for solidity.

You can draw any site inside the browser. I drew [my little blog](/en/). In troubled times, a good old separate website with [RSS](/en/posts/147/) will stay accessible a bit longer than everything else. Save the link for yourself if you like it. Good luck to you and safe internets.
